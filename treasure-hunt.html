The Aqaba Old Town Treasure Hunt

Five real landmarks. Five local clues. Find them all and unlock a discount on your next Suhaila experience.

This free hunt takes you on foot through Aqaba's old town — the same streets our Walking Tour and Cooking Class guests explore. Read each clue, walk to the spot in real life, then tap "Reveal" to confirm you found it and learn the story behind it. No booking, no sign-up — just wander, discover, and see you at the finish line.

0 / 5 landmarks found

1 The Old Fortress

"Look for thick stone walls that have watched over this port since the 1500s. Sultans built me to guard pilgrims heading to Mecca — find the fortress with cannons still facing the sea."

Aqaba Fort (Mamluk Castle)

Built by the Mamluks in the 16th century, this fort later played a role in the famous 1917 Arab Revolt — the same events immortalized in "Lawrence of Arabia."

2 The House of Prayer

"From the fort, walk toward the golden call to prayer. I'm named after the Sharif who led a revolt, and my minaret rises near the seafront promenade."

Sharif Hussein Bin Ali Mosque

Named after Sharif Hussein bin Ali, who launched the Great Arab Revolt of 1916 from this very region of Jordan.

3 Scented Alleys

"Follow your nose into the alleys where sacks of cardamom, sumac, and za'atar spill onto the street. Bargain like a local for the best blend in town."

The Spice Souk

Za'atar, a wild thyme blend, has flavored Levantine kitchens for centuries — grab some here, it's exactly what goes into Suhaila's cooking class recipes.

4 Catch of the Day

"Nearby, the morning's Red Sea catch glistens on ice. Follow the smell of the sea and the shouting fishmongers to find it."

The Fish Souk

Aqaba's fishing fleet still brings in the day's catch every morning — much of what ends up in Suhaila's Sayadieh comes straight from here.

5 Where the City Meets the Sea

"End your hunt where fishing boats bob beside pleasure yachts, and the Red Sea stretches out toward four countries at once."

Aqaba Marina

From this spot on a clear day, you can see four countries touching the same gulf: Jordan, Israel, Egypt, and Saudi Arabia.
