Olive Harvest in Jordan: Picking Season and the Journey to the Press

Published on October 18, 2026 · 5 min read

When autumn settles over Jordan, olive season brings a different kind of energy to villages and family farms. The harvest is more than a job to finish before the weather changes. It is a reason for relatives to gather, share food, catch up beneath the trees, and turn a year's care into olive oil for the home.

When is olive-picking season in Jordan?

The harvest usually takes place in autumn, often from October into November, but the exact timing changes from year to year. Variety, location, weather, and the ripeness growers want all play a part. Some olives are picked while still green; others are left to darken on the tree. If you are visiting, ask local growers or the mill whether harvesting is underway nearby rather than relying on a fixed date.

A family day among the olive trees

Harvest morning often starts early. Nets are spread beneath the branches to catch the fruit, then family members work their way through the trees, picking by hand or using simple combs and tools. The olives are gathered from the nets, leaves are separated out, and the harvest is collected into crates or sacks.

There is usually plenty happening alongside the picking: tea poured between rows, bread and little dishes brought out for a break, children carrying a small basket, and relatives calling to one another from the next tree. The work can be tiring, but doing it together is part of what makes the season memorable.

From the grove to the olive press

Once picked, olives are best taken to the mill promptly. At the press, they are cleaned and washed before being crushed into a paste. The paste is mixed so the tiny oil droplets can come together, then processed to separate the oil from water and the remaining olive material. Mills use different equipment and methods, but the goal is the same: handle the fruit carefully and extract the oil while it is fresh.

For families, taking the harvest to the mill is its own seasonal outing. Crates wait their turn, the machinery hums, and everyone keeps an eye out for the first stream of oil. The fresh oil is often tasted simply, with bread, so its grassy aroma and peppery finish can speak for themselves.

The first taste of the season

At home, new olive oil needs little fuss. Dip warm bread into a small dish of oil and za'atar, drizzle it over hummus, or spoon it onto a plate of tomatoes and herbs. In a Jordanian kitchen, olive oil is not only an ingredient; it is part of the welcome offered to anyone sitting at the table.

Olive season is a reminder that food carries the stories of the people who grow it. If you get the chance to visit a grove or mill, go with someone local, ask before taking photographs, and follow the grower's guidance around equipment and working areas. The best souvenir may be a taste of fresh oil and a little understanding of the care behind it.
